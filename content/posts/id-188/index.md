title: "Business or ethics?"
---

Drawing the line between money and ethics.

In an era where newspapers and magazines are no longer the only sources of news for the wider public, and where the existence of social media has resulted in an outpour of independent journalism, the question arises: where is the line between financial interests and journalistic ethics?

Let us look at a couple of examples.

One issue that highlights the dilemma between sincere morality and the will to acquire financially is choosing whether or not to publish a piece of information given to any journalist/reporter in confidentiality. Breaching the confidentiality rights of an individual is widely accepted as an offence, but there is the argument that without it, a lot of big stories in the public's interest will be unheard of.

One could argue that if journalists are really interested in the public's safety and interest, there could be other, more ethical ways to find out about it such as requesting Freedom of Information or formally contacting the individual to arrange an interview. But if it was something that could cause anyone harm, would it not be a crime to stay silent whilst knowing about it? It's all about what is considered higher priority.

This therefore proves itself to be a major aspect that lies in the public interest as the lives of many people could be affected negatively. So although it is a breach of privacy law, the journalist’s priority could be the people and therefore aim to expose something that could otherwise cost the lives of others.

Another instance is where journalists 'bend the truth' in order to attract traffic to their articles, for example clickbait. There could be headlines such as 'the shocking and heartbreaking truth about Alicia Keys,' when the actual article itself isn't nearly half as exaggerated, and is probably about her breaking a nail or something.

Readers are often very disappointed by such overly exaggerated headlines, which are lies to an extent, as they find it a waste of time. Proper journalism is truthful and straightforward with no strings attached, all else is bad journalism. When a journalist constantly uses clickbait, not only are they seen as desperate to gain some clicks, but they are also ruining their future chances of keeping those viewers.

And although some newspapers such as The Sun make lots of money, they are notorious for holding no value for ethics and no impartiality.

So the choice is such: money, or ethics?
